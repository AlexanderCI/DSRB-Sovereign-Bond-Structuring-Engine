# DSRB alignment brief: SDRB 2026-1

## Deal thesis

SDRB 2026-1 is a synthetic USD 5.0bn structured-finance transaction for allied defence and resilience infrastructure. The deal takes a pool of sovereign-backed loans and turns it into a tranched capital-markets instrument with senior notes, mezzanine notes, and a first-loss equity / excess spread piece.

The point is not just to make a bond. The point is to make a funding format that lets a DSRB-style institution bring private capital into defence and resilience without leaving investors blind on cashflow priority, tail loss, or credit enhancement.

## Why DSRB would care

DSRB sits in an unusual lane. It is meant to support security-related funding, but the assets do not always fit neatly into normal green, infrastructure, export-credit, or plain sovereign bond buckets.

This structure helps because it can:

- convert many sovereign-backed project loans into one investable note format
- protect senior investors with subordination, OC, reserve cash, and a multilateral guarantee
- leave first-loss risk with a controlled equity / support tranche
- give Treasury a visible WACC and duration profile
- give Risk a path-level loss and trigger-breach distribution
- give DCM / syndicate a real story for investor placement

## Collateral framing

The synthetic portfolio includes:

- defence logistics facilities
- dual-use port infrastructure
- cyber-resilience grids
- secure cloud and data-centre facilities
- allied supply-chain manufacturing
- satellite ground-station networks

These are the kind of assets where sovereign importance can be high, but private financing still needs structure. A pension fund or insurer will not simply buy “defence resilience exposure” unless the waterfall, credit support, and loss analytics are clean.

## Capital stack logic

The senior tranche is meant to clear the largest investor base. It receives first cashflow priority and has subordination underneath it. A DSRB first-loss guarantee wraps a defined senior loss layer, aiming to reduce expected senior loss and support a synthetic AAA-style credit profile.

The mezzanine tranche takes more spread and absorbs losses after equity is hit. It is the piece that can appeal to private credit accounts or specialist structured-credit funds.

The equity tranche is not treated as a vague residual. It is modelled as first-loss capital with excess spread upside, PDL exposure, and VaR reporting. That matters because this is where the real risk sits.

## Waterfall discipline

The payment order is strict:

1. senior fees
2. senior interest
3. mezzanine interest
4. senior principal amortisation
5. mezzanine principal amortisation
6. reserve and trapped cash mechanics
7. equity excess spread

The engine also cures senior and mezzanine PDLs before equity can receive normal distributions. That is basic structured-credit discipline, but it is exactly what makes the transaction investor-readable.

## Risk analytics

The risk model produces:

- monthly portfolio collections
- gross losses
- collateral roll-down
- senior and mezzanine PDL paths
- OC trigger breaches
- senior expected loss before and after guarantee
- equity tranche VaR
- tranche IRR and duration

This gives Treasury and Risk the same language. Treasury sees funding cost and duration. Risk sees PDLs, tail loss, and OC deterioration. Front office sees tranche thickness, coupon, and investor hurdle clearance.

## Front-office use case

A structurer could use this engine to answer the real questions before a mandate goes to market:

- How much senior paper can we issue without weakening subordination too much?
- What is the cheapest WACC that still clears investor yield hurdles?
- How much guarantee notional is actually needed to wrap senior risk?
- Does excess spread protect the stack or leak to equity too early?
- What happens to equity IRR under severe regional escalation?
- Are OC triggers actually doing work, or just sitting there as decoration?


## Obvious extensions

A real DSRB desk would add:

- country concentration limits
- eligibility and covenant screens
- delayed recovery vectors
- rating-agency stress grids
- legal maturity and call features
- investor allocation and syndicate book feedback
- sovereign guarantee enforceability assumptions
- FX basis and cross-currency funding costs
- sanctions and procurement eligibility filters

Those are deliberately not hidden. They are the next layers after the core structuring engine is built.
